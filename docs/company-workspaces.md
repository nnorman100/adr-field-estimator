## Roles and source of authorization

Verified Firebase ID tokens from Google or email/password sign-in establish identity. With WORKSPACES_ENABLED=true, Firestore memberships authorize access; browser capabilities and Firebase custom claims cannot grant permissions. The root user is bootstrapped by an explicit migration, not by matching an email on each request. Superadmin membership lives separately from company membership.

| Role | Allowed operations |
| --- | --- |
| Superadmin | Create/suspend/reactivate companies, invite or replace owners (or create an owner's login with a temporary password), inspect account/invitation metadata. Read-only support access to any company's jobs, drafts, signed agreements, documents, photos, team and invitations, plus support actions: resend or cancel a staff invitation, switch a non-owner member between estimator and office, re-enable a disabled member. Every support view and action is audited. No job, document, profile or other company writes through this role. |
| Owner | Company profile/team/invitations; all company jobs, coordination, assignments and the full field workflow. |
| Estimator | Jobs created by them or currently assigned to them; Live, scope (including offline voice notes), photos/OCR, audits and customer-signature capture. Reassignment retains creator access. |
| Office | Company jobs and documents/evidence/export, including the read-only drying log; empty-scope job creation; coordination and assignments. The team endpoint returns office staff only active estimators' uid/email for assignment; estimators cannot read the team. Scope, capture, OCR, audits and signature submission are denied. |

One active owner and one company membership per user are supported. Superadmin support access is not a membership: it does not use the user's one membership, cannot be assigned jobs and grants no capability. A superadmin who is also a company member keeps that membership's permissions; support access covers only what the membership does not, such as other companies or the full roster for an office member. Office-created jobs have no rooms or measured scope; the estimator supplies them. Signed property/document contents are immutable; office contact notes and assignment sidecars can still change. Owners cannot replace or disable the active owner; superadmin replacement takes effect atomically on acceptance (or when a superadmin creates the new owner's login directly) and disables the prior membership. A disabled former owner can later be given a staff role.

Private collections are _companies, _memberships, _platform_admins, _invitations, _invite_keys, _admin_events, _company_limits, _workspace_jobs and _workspace_authorizations. _admin_events is also the support audit log (see Superadmin support access). Job sidecars contain company, creator, assignee, coordination version and query summaries. Signed source records, payload hashes and stored evidence paths are retained. The migration manifest records source fingerprints around each run to detect concurrent source changes before cutover. Every write rechecks permission within its transaction; long AI results recheck before commit and evidence downloads before return. Live rechecks permission and account revocation every 20 seconds, ends the session at the first recheck after access is lost, sends nothing further to the model, removes checkpoints and refuses a resume. Existing per-user quotas/leases remain. Each company may create or resend at most 50 invitations per day; more return 429 with Retry-After.

## Superadmin support access

Superadmins can open any company read-only to support its owner and troubleshoot. In the dashboard, **Companies → Manage company → Open read-only support view** lists the company's drafts and signed agreements, its team and its invitations. Opening a job shows its rooms, coordination details, generated documents, photos and, for signed agreements, the signature. The view offers no forms or edit controls. It shows only the support actions the server allows: **Change to estimator / office staff** and **Re-enable access** for non-owner members other than the superadmin, and **Resend / Cancel invitation** for pending invitations. Both require an active company. Owner replacement stays on the company administration page: an owner invitation, or an owner login with a temporary password (see below).

Support reads use GET routes under /api/admin/companies/{id}: jobs, jobs/{inspection_id}, jobs/{inspection_id}/photos/{photo_id}, authorizations, authorizations/{auth_id} and authorizations/{auth_id}/evidence/{name}. Team and invitations use the existing /api/companies/{id}/team and invitations routes. Support actions use the existing team PUT and invitation resend/cancel routes. Every request repeats the revoked-account check. The routes exist only with WORKSPACES_ENABLED=true. In legacy owner-only mode they return 503 like the other workspace routes. A superadmin still has no access through the member routes (/api/inspections, /api/authorizations, coordination, audit, OCR, signing and signing links) or the company profile PUT, unless they hold a membership there. Support creates no staff invitations and cannot disable members, change an owner's or its own membership, or edit jobs, scope, photos, OCR, audits, pricing, signatures or coordination.

Each support read checks superadmin status before reading. It then rechecks superadmin status, the company and, for a single record, that the job belongs to that company, and writes its audit entry in the same transaction before anything is returned. Photo and evidence downloads therefore recheck after the download, and a superadmin revoked mid-read gets 403 and leaves no entry. Support actions record their entry in the same transaction as the change. Entries are created once in `_admin_events` and never rewritten: `actor_uid`, `company_id`, `action` (`support_view_company`, `support_view_jobs`, `support_view_documents`, `support_view_job`, `support_view_document`, `support_view_photo`, `support_view_evidence`, `support_view_team`, `support_view_invitations`, `support_member_access`, `support_invitation_resend`, `support_invitation_cancel`), `target` (record IDs only, such as `job/<inspection id>` or `member/<uid>`) and `created_at`. Member changes also record the new role and status. Entries never contain customer names, contact details, emails or job contents. Browser database access to the log stays denied by firestore.rules; there is no in-app log viewer yet.

Every superadmin tool refuses any caller without an active `_platform_admins` record with 403, before reading the target, so a refused caller cannot probe which companies exist. That includes owners, office staff, estimators, disabled members, members of suspended companies, accounts without a membership and inactive superadmins. Refused requests return no data, make no change and write no audit entry. Firebase custom claims, extra request fields and request headers cannot grant superadmin status. `tests/test_workspaces.py` holds this refusal matrix, and its route inventory check fails when an `/api/admin` route is added without a matrix entry.

## Owner logins with a temporary password

A superadmin can create a company owner's login directly instead of sending an owner invitation. In **Companies**, **Create a company** offers **Owner sign-in: Create login with temporary password**, and **Manage company** offers **Create login with temporary password** beside **Invite / replace owner**. The superadmin enters the owner's email (the username) and a temporary password they type, of 12 to 128 characters. The field is masked; **Show** reveals it to check before creating the login, and **Hide** masks it again. After creation the page shows the email and that password once with **Copy password**, even if refreshing the company page afterwards fails, but only while the account that submitted the form is still signed in; if the browser switched accounts meanwhile (for example in another tab), it shows nothing. A sign-in change also removes a shown card and clears typed temporary passwords, and while it is still applying the form refuses to submit with "Your sign-in changed. Reload and try again." Switching **Create a company** back to **Email an invitation** clears the temporary password. The app sends no email: the superadmin sends the sign-in from their own email. Owners keep using invitations for estimators and office staff.

`POST /api/admin/companies` with `owner_password`, or `POST /api/admin/companies/{id}/owner-login` with `email`, `password` and the company's `expected_version`, does this in one guarded operation:

1. It refuses any caller who is not an active superadmin, before any lookup, so a refused request touches no Firebase account and writes no record or audit entry. The `/api/admin` refusal matrix in `tests/test_workspaces.py` covers both routes.
2. It refuses, with a message and no change, an email that already has a company membership, a Google account (with or without a password), a disabled Firebase account, a password outside 12 to 128 characters, a suspended or unknown company and a stale `expected_version`.
3. A transaction reserves the account in `_owner_login_reservations` (keyed by the email's SHA-256, holding a random claim and a 600-second expiry). It refuses an account that already has a membership (including one from an accepted invitation) or an unexpired reservation from another owner login. While the reservation stands, invitation acceptance for that email returns 409, so no competing request can take the account between the password write and the membership commit. Any failure after this step deletes the request's own reservation; an abandoned one expires. The expiry outlasts the request's Firebase writes, so a slow write cannot land after another owner login reused the account: those writes go through a separate Firebase app (`adr-owner-login`) with a 10-second HTTP timeout and SDK retries turned off (the SDK would otherwise retry 500 and 503 responses and honor Retry-After waits of up to six hours). Each call is at most three rounds of a token fetch and the request (google-auth retries a 401 twice), each a 10-second connect and read wait, so at most 120 seconds; an owner login makes at most two calls, 240 seconds, well inside 600. A transient Firebase error therefore fails the request instead of retrying it, and the superadmin tries again. An unexpected Firebase or database failure, such as the missing permission below, answers 503 "The owner login could not be created. Try again or contact support."; the temporary-password change answers 503 "Your password could not be changed. Try again." Other routes keep the generic draft-retry message.
4. It then creates the email/password account in Firebase Authentication, or sets the password on an existing account without a membership (such as one an owner invitation provisioned), marks the email verified because the superadmin vouches for it, and sets the custom claim `password_change_required`. A new account is created unverified and becomes verified in the same update that sets the claim, so it is never usable without the claim. Setting a password ends the account's other sessions.
5. One transaction then rechecks superadmin status, that the reservation is still this request's, the company's status and version and that the account still has no membership, and deletes the reservation when it commits. It makes the account the active owner with the semantics of an accepted owner invitation: the prior owner's membership is disabled, pending owner invitations and pending invitations to either owner's email are cancelled, and the company's `owner_uid` and version change. An owner invitation accepted in between makes this a 409 conflict; the Firebase account then holds the temporary password but grants nothing, and a retry with the new version reuses it.
6. The same transaction appends an `owner_login_created` entry to `_admin_events`: the superadmin's uid, the company, `target` `member/<uid>`, and `details` with the masked email (first character and domain, such as `n***@example.com`), `replacement`, `previous_owner_uid` and the number of cancelled invitations. It is the one admin entry that holds an email, and only masked.

The password goes only to Firebase Authentication. It is never logged, stored in Firestore, put in an audit entry or returned in a response; validation errors (422) carry each problem's message and location but never the submitted values, and the response carries only the company and the owner's uid and email.

**Must change password.** The new membership carries `password_change_required: true`, the server's gate. Until it clears, the membership grants nothing: the account has no role, capabilities or superadmin support access, and every session, job, workspace, signing-link and Live route answers 403 "Set your own password before using DryScope." (Live refuses before any model connection). Only `POST /api/account/password` is open to it. The `password_change_required` custom claim is a denial condition too: every route except the password change refuses a token carrying it with the same 403, before any membership lookup, so an account whose membership step failed after Firebase took the temporary password cannot accept an invitation or use any other route with it. The browser reads the custom claim: after sign-in with the claim, the app shows only **Set your own password** (new password twice, at least 12 characters) and makes no session, job or workspace calls. If the token predates the claim, the session's 403 shows the same step. On save, the backend sets the new password and removes the claim in Firebase Authentication first, then clears the membership marker and appends `temporary_password_replaced`; the app reauthenticates the same account with the new password and opens the owner's dashboard. Because the backend sets the password itself, a modified client cannot clear the gate without changing it. If Firebase fails, the gate stays and the owner retries. If the request fails after Firebase may have taken the new password (a 5xx or a lost response), the app tries reauthenticating the same account with the new password; when that works, it resumes with that sign-in, and if the server's marker is still set the owner saves once more to finish. If the browser signed in to another account while the save was pending (for example in another tab), the app opens nothing and leaves that sign-in alone. The route requires a password sign-in and either the marker or the claim, so an account whose membership step failed can still choose a password; once set, a password changes only through **Forgot password**, which keeps working for these accounts. Forgot password before the change does not lift the gate: the owner still sets a password in the app.

Release needs the runtime role to hold `firebaseauth.users.update` (see [firebase-isolation.md](firebase-isolation.md)).

## Sign-in methods

With WORKSPACES_ENABLED=true the backend accepts Firebase ID tokens whose sign-in provider is `google.com` or `password`, and only when the email is verified. Password accounts are created by invitation or, for owners, by a superadmin with a temporary password (see above). The app has no sign-up control. Before each invitation email is sent, the backend looks up the invited email in Firebase Authentication and creates an unverified account without a password if none exists. This stops anyone else from registering the invited address first. If the account does not sign in with Google, the email carries a set-password link; otherwise it tells the invitee to sign in with Google using that email. Resending an invitation reuses the account and mails a fresh link. The link is a credential, so it appears only in the outgoing email, never in Firestore or logs. If the link has expired, the invitee uses **Forgot password** on the sign-in page. An unverified password account stays on the verification step and makes no session or job calls. A verified account still has no access until it accepts a company invitation for that exact email. The legacy STAFF_EMAILS mode is unchanged.

Public sign-up must stay disabled, or the Firebase Auth API still accepts self-registration with the public web API key. It was disabled on October 10, 2026 (see `docs/review-repairs.md`) and read back as disabled on October 14, 2026. Re-applying it is a separate production change. Run it only with operator approval:

```
PATCH https://identitytoolkit.googleapis.com/admin/v2/projects/alert-disaster-field-2026/config?updateMask=client.permissions.disabledUserSignup
{"client":{"permissions":{"disabledUserSignup":true}}}
```

Verify it by reading `GET https://identitytoolkit.googleapis.com/admin/v2/projects/alert-disaster-field-2026/config` and confirming `client.permissions.disabledUserSignup` is `true`. While sign-up is disabled, a client sign-up attempt returns `auth/admin-restricted-operation`, which the app shows as "Ask your company owner for an invitation." Server-side account creation through the Admin SDK is not affected.

Identity Platform request logging is on (`monitoring.requestLogging.enabled=true`). It was enabled on October 16, 2026, with operator approval, after a password-reset email arrived that no log could attribute: until then Firebase Auth recorded no sign-in, sign-up or reset request anywhere. Each Identity Toolkit request, including `sendOobCode` password-reset requests sent from **Forgot password** or straight to the public API, is now written to Cloud Logging in `alert-disaster-field-2026`. Each entry records the request method and its outcome together with the email address involved and the caller's IP address, so these logs hold personal data; read them only to investigate sign-in, reset or account-security events. The change was applied alone with `PATCH …/config?updateMask=monitoring.requestLogging.enabled` and `{"monitoring":{"requestLogging":{"enabled":true}}}`, and a read-back confirmed every other config field was unchanged. Turning it off is a separate approved production change with the same mask and `false`.

**Forgot password** asks "Send a password reset link to name@…?" before it sends anything, because it sits just above **Sign in with Google** and a stray tap would otherwise email a reset link.

The app runs only on its canonical host. A host written with a trailing dot (`alert-disaster-field-2026.web.app.`) is a separate browser origin that Firebase Auth refuses for Google sign-in (`auth/unauthorized-domain`) and the Live relay refuses (HTTP 403), so the app and the signing page reload on the same URL without the dot before Firebase starts. A Home Screen app added from the dotted address stays on that origin, and iOS opens the canonical page in a sheet above it, so the app also tells the user to delete the icon and add it again from the correct address. Local drafts are stored per origin, so while any draft on the dotted address has not synced the app stays there, shows that drafts are still syncing and to keep the icon, and moves to the canonical host once the queue is empty.

Firebase keeps one account per email. Signing in with Google replaces an unverified email/password account with the same address, and some combinations return "This email already uses a different sign-in method." Each staff member should use one sign-in method per email. The app has no account-linking flow.

`firebase.json` enables the email/password provider. The project already has it enabled alongside Google (read back on October 14, 2026), so activation needs no auth deploy. Re-applying the `firebase.json` providers is a separate approved step: `firebase deploy --only auth --project alert-disaster-field-2026`. The Firebase CLI auth deploy only enables providers; it also re-sends the existing Google provider settings from `firebase.json`.

## Invitations and interfaces

Superadmin creates companies or sends owner replacements. Owners invite estimator/office emails; superadmin support can resend or cancel those invitations but not create them. Invitations last seven days and activate only after explicit acceptance by a verified account with the exact invited email. Acceptance checks current inviter access and company status. Owner invitations bind the owner being replaced so competing invitations cannot silently replace a newly accepted owner. Repeated acceptance is idempotent; incompatible existing membership, expiry and cancellation return conflicts. Team changes use expected versions.

Invitation email uses one of two server-only transports, chosen by `INVITATION_TRANSPORT`: exactly `resend` (the default when unset or empty) or `smtp` (Gmail with a Google app password). Both send the same subject (`Invitation to join <company name> on DryScope`), text and HTML, with up to three attempts on transient failures. The HTML is branded with the company's name and its Company settings logo (without one, the DryScope logo, or ADR's logo for ADR; both are bundled in `backend/brand/` because the build context is `backend/`), says what DryScope is, who sent it and that the recipient can ignore an unexpected invitation. The logo travels as an inline attachment (`content_id` for Resend, `multipart/related` for SMTP), never as a data URL or hosted image; company text is HTML-escaped. Send failures are visible; operators can resend or cancel. A send reserves its attempt for 30 seconds, and concurrent sends within that window reuse it. Attempts older than 23 hours require explicit resend, which keeps Resend inside its idempotency window. Any other transport value (including different capitalization or surrounding spaces) or a missing setting fails the delivery before any account is provisioned.

- **Resend** needs `INVITATION_FROM` (a sender on a domain verified in Resend) and `RESEND_API_KEY`. Each attempt has an eight-second transport deadline. Each attempt carries the idempotency key `<invitation id>/<send attempt>`, so Resend suppresses a duplicate when a retry repeats a request it already accepted.
- **Gmail SMTP** connects to `smtp.gmail.com` on port 587 and upgrades with STARTTLS before signing in. Each network operation has an eight-second timeout, and the whole send, from the start of account provisioning through every attempt and pause, stops after 24 seconds, inside the 30-second reservation, so a concurrent send cannot open a second Gmail session for the same attempt. If account provisioning uses up that time, the delivery fails without contacting Gmail and the operator can resend. It needs `INVITATION_FROM`, `INVITATION_SMTP_USERNAME` (the Gmail address) and `INVITATION_SMTP_PASSWORD` (the app password). Set `INVITATION_FROM` to that same Gmail address, for example `DryScope <sender@example.com>`; Gmail rewrites any other From address unless it is a verified "Send mail as" alias. At release, an operator changes the display name in the production `INVITATION_FROM` secret to DryScope; this change does not update it. SMTP has no idempotency key. Each attempt sets the deterministic Message-ID `<invitation-<invitation id>-<send attempt>@<sender domain>>`, which is the stored delivery id. Gmail does not deduplicate on it, so duplicate suppression is weaker than Resend's: if a connection fails after Gmail accepted the message, the retry can deliver a second copy. Mail clients may group the copies by their shared Message-ID. Google limits a personal Gmail account to about 500 recipients per day and may block sending for up to 24 hours after that. The app's own cap of 50 invitations per company per day sits below that limit for a single company.

Before switching to Gmail, the Google account owner turns on 2-Step Verification, then creates an app password for that account at `https://myaccount.google.com/apppasswords`. The owner stores it in Secret Manager themselves as a dedicated secret (in alert-disaster-field-2026 it is named `ADRfieldappinvitations`), never in chat, source, CLI arguments or artifacts. Bind it to the service as the environment variable `INVITATION_SMTP_PASSWORD`. Changing the Google account password revokes app passwords, so a new one must replace the secret version. To switch to Resend later, verify a sending domain in Resend, store `RESEND_API_KEY` in its own secret and bind it, set `INVITATION_FROM` to a sender on that domain, and set `INVITATION_TRANSPORT=resend` (or remove it). The SMTP settings can then be removed and the app password revoked.

The invitation link is an opaque invitation ID; the ID alone grants no account access. Account provisioning needs the backend runtime identity to read and create Firebase Authentication users and generate password-reset links. The runtime account holds the custom role `adrInviteProvisioner` (`firebaseauth.users.get`, `firebaseauth.users.create`, `firebaseauth.users.sendEmail`) for this; the first real invitation on the staged revision is the live confirmation. No custom-token grant is required. A failed lookup, creation or link generation marks the delivery failed; **Resend invitation** retries it.

The additive interfaces are /api/access; /api/admin/companies and company status; the read-only support routes under /api/admin/companies/{id} (jobs, authorizations, photos and evidence); /api/companies/{id} profile, team and invitations; /api/invitations/{id} read/accept; /api/admin/companies/{id}/owner-login and /api/account/password; and /api/jobs/{inspection_id}/coordination. Existing inspection and agreement URLs, the offline voice-note route /api/inspections/{id}/voice-notes (scope capability) and the drying-log routes /api/inspections/{id}/readings (GET with jobs_read; POST and DELETE /readings/{reading_id} with the scope capability, allowed on signed jobs because readings are a sidecar document, never part of the signed record) use company permissions. Session responses advertise workspace_protocol_version=1 and role/capabilities. Company-aware clients send X-ADR-Workspace-Protocol: 1. New company audits/signatures and saved branded documents require this header, preventing cached clients from displaying hardcoded ADR identity. Every Live handshake must also send workspace_protocol_version=1 while workspaces are enabled; older clients, including ADR's, receive 409 and recover through the update banner. Historical unsigned/legacy records retain compatibility.

## Company documents and offline drafts

Company profiles provide name (up to 80 letters, numbers, spaces or . , & ' ( ) - characters, with no web address, because invitation emails include it), address/contact, CSLB license, sign-off and optional validated PNG/JPEG logo. This release is California-only and uses the shared existing residential workbook rates/formulas/internal scope codes. Pricing is server-owned. ADR notices remain exact; standard clauses that name the company use the company's own name. Missing company identity blocks signing and is shown in review. A homeowner signing link acts as the owner or estimator who sent it: the homeowner page shows the company's own letterhead, signing writes the same company records as on-device signing, and the link stops working if that person loses the job's `sign` permission (see [firebase-isolation.md](firebase-isolation.md#homeowner-signing-links)).

New audits store company_identity and its profile version. Profile changes invalidate unsigned signability until regeneration. New audits also store the coordinated customer name, which the proposal, itemized claim and defense letter show; a blank name prints no customer line. Saving a different customer name clears an unsigned job's documents, and signing refuses documents whose saved name no longer matches coordination. Documents audited before customer names were stored sign and render unchanged. Signed documents retain the saved identity; records without one render from the backend's `LEGACY_IDENTITY` (ADR's historical letterhead, sent as `legacy_identity` in the session and in homeowner signing-link views), never a live profile. Company-supplied labels are untrusted JSON data in model context and text nodes in the browser.

Estimators land on My jobs, office staff on Company jobs, and owners/superadmins on their dashboard. Signing in opens the workspace without creating or automatically reopening an inspection. Authorized local drafts remain available through Jobs.

Draft keys include user/company/inspection. Legacy local drafts recover only after online confirmation of ADR membership. Access/context changes stop capture, clear visible records, pause queued writes and retain local drafts for authorized recovery. Offline coordination/admin mutation controls are disabled. Cached role data never authorizes a server operation; online synchronization revalidates access.
