## Roles and source of authorization

Verified Firebase ID tokens from Google or email/password sign-in establish identity. With WORKSPACES_ENABLED=true, Firestore memberships authorize access; browser capabilities and Firebase custom claims cannot grant permissions. The root user is bootstrapped by an explicit migration, not by matching an email on each request. Superadmin membership lives separately from company membership.

| Role | Allowed operations |
| --- | --- |
| Superadmin | Create/suspend/reactivate companies, invite or replace owners (or create an owner's login with a temporary password), inspect account/invitation metadata. Read-only support access to any company's jobs, drafts, signed agreements, documents, photos, team and invitations, plus support actions: resend or cancel a staff invitation, switch a non-owner member between estimator and office, re-enable a disabled member, and archive or restore an unsigned draft. Every support view and action is audited. No other job, document, profile or company writes through this role. |
| Owner | Company profile/team/invitations; all company jobs, coordination, assignments, copying a signed job to a new draft, address suggestions and the job map, and the full field workflow. |
| Estimator | Jobs created by them or currently assigned to them; Live, scope (including offline voice notes), photos/OCR, audits and customer-signature capture. Reassignment retains creator access. They can copy a signed job they can open to a new draft and use address suggestions and the job map (the `places` capability, below). |
| Office | Company jobs and documents/evidence/export, including the read-only drying log; empty-scope job creation; copying a signed job to a new draft; coordination and assignments. The team endpoint returns office staff only active estimators' uid/email for assignment; estimators cannot read the team. Scope, capture, OCR, audits and signature submission are denied. |

Address suggestions and the job map (`/api/places/autocomplete`, `/details` and `/map`) need the `places` capability, held by owners and estimators because they edit the address field; office staff, superadmin support access and other companies' users without the role are refused by the server, and a lookup carries no job, so it reads nothing from any company. Office staff and the support view still see the job's keyless directions link but no map image. The caps and key handling are in [firebase-isolation.md](firebase-isolation.md#address-suggestions-and-the-job-map). Drafts keep `coordinates` in the saved inspection; older jobs have none.

One active owner and one company membership per user are supported. Superadmin support access is not a membership: it does not use the user's one membership, cannot be assigned jobs and grants no capability. A superadmin who is also a company member keeps that membership's permissions; support access covers only what the membership does not, such as other companies or the full roster for an office member. Jobs office staff start from scratch have no rooms or measured scope; the estimator supplies them. For the copy action in the app, see [README](../README.md). Copying a signed job creates a new draft with the rooms, scope and claim details, records `copied_from` (the signed agreement and inspection IDs) and copies no signatures, audit, signing links, photos, readings, transcripts, scanned tags or customer contact details; the signed record is not touched. Older signed jobs without a valid saved inspection cannot be copied; start a new inspection instead. The creating transaction rechecks the caller's role and access to the source job. Signed property/document contents are immutable; office contact notes and assignment sidecars can still change. Owners cannot replace or disable the active owner; superadmin replacement takes effect atomically on acceptance (or when a superadmin creates the new owner's login directly) and disables the prior membership. A disabled former owner can later be given a staff role.

Private collections are _companies, _memberships, _platform_admins, _invitations, _invite_keys, _admin_events, _company_limits, _workspace_jobs, _workspace_authorizations and _migrations. _admin_events is also the support audit log (see Superadmin support access). Job sidecars contain company, creator, assignee, coordination version, query summaries and the job's claim and loss details (`claim`, with its own `claim_version`; jobs saved before claim details have neither and read as none recorded). Signed source records, payload hashes and stored evidence paths are retained. The migration manifest records source fingerprints around each run to detect concurrent source changes before cutover. Every write rechecks permission within its transaction; long AI results recheck before commit and evidence downloads before return. Live rechecks permission and account revocation every 20 seconds, ends the session at the first recheck after access is lost, sends nothing further to the model, removes checkpoints and refuses a resume. Existing per-user quotas/leases remain. Each company may create or resend at most 50 invitations per day; more return 429 with Retry-After.

## Superadmin support access

Superadmins can open any company read-only to support its owner and troubleshoot. In the dashboard, **Companies → Manage company → Open read-only support view** lists the company's drafts and signed agreements, its team and its invitations. Opening a job shows its rooms, coordination details, generated documents, photos and, for signed agreements, the signature. Job records have no edit controls; list filtering is described under [Company documents and offline drafts](#company-documents-and-offline-drafts). It shows only the support actions the server allows: **Change to estimator / office staff** and **Re-enable access** for non-owner members other than the superadmin, and **Resend / Cancel invitation** for pending invitations. These actions require an active company. An unsigned draft's page also has **Archive draft (support action)**, and **Archived drafts** below the job list restores one. These two work in a suspended company too, so a test company whose owner cannot sign in can still be cleaned up (`POST /api/admin/companies/{id}/jobs/{inspection_id}/archive` and `/restore`, `GET /api/admin/companies/{id}/archived-jobs`). Owners have the same **Archived drafts** list on their job list (`GET /api/archived-jobs`, `POST /api/jobs/{inspection_id}/restore`). Owner replacement stays on the company administration page: an owner invitation, or an owner login with a temporary password (see below).

Photo locations are saved by the same photo upload that needs the scope capability, so no new permission exists. They are visible in the superadmin read-only support view and the signing page's photo log. See [Photo locations](../README.md#inspection-workflow) for capture, offline, older-photo and privacy behavior, and `PhotoLocation` in `backend/contracts.py` for the metadata contract. Coordinates are job content like addresses: logs carry route templates and field names only.

Support reads use GET routes under /api/admin/companies/{id}: jobs, jobs/{inspection_id}, jobs/{inspection_id}/photos/{photo_id}, authorizations, authorizations/{auth_id} and authorizations/{auth_id}/evidence/{name}. Team and invitations use the existing /api/companies/{id}/team and invitations routes. Support actions use the existing team PUT and invitation resend/cancel routes. Every request repeats the revoked-account check. The routes exist only with WORKSPACES_ENABLED=true. In legacy owner-only mode they return 503 like the other workspace routes. A superadmin still has no access through the member routes (/api/inspections, /api/authorizations, coordination, audit, OCR, signing and signing links) or the company profile PUT, unless they hold a membership there. Support creates no staff invitations and cannot disable members, change an owner's or its own membership, or edit jobs, scope, photos, OCR, audits, pricing, signatures or coordination.

Before an owner removes a team member's access, by Remove access or by saving Access: Disabled, the confirmation lists that member's pending signing links (`GET /api/companies/{id}/team/{member_uid}/signing-links`, owner only, read-only, never the token): jobs whose link could still sign (unexpired, unused and unrevoked, the job unsigned and its estimate unchanged since the link was created, and the member still able to sign that job), with customer name and address and the link's creation time. A link that is already dead stays out of the list even while its record still says `pending`. The listing is informational and uses no rate-limit quota; if it cannot load, the confirmation says so and the owner can still remove access. Removing access does not touch the link records. The link signs as its technician, so it stops working (the homeowner sees the usual unavailable message) and stays `pending` until it expires.

Each support read checks superadmin status before reading. It then rechecks superadmin status, the company and, for a single record, that the job belongs to that company, and writes its audit entry in the same transaction before anything is returned. Photo and evidence downloads therefore recheck after the download, and a superadmin revoked mid-read gets 403 and leaves no entry. Support actions record their entry in the same transaction as the change. Entries are created once in `_admin_events` and never rewritten: `actor_uid`, `company_id`, `action` (`support_view_company`, `support_view_jobs`, `support_view_documents`, `support_view_job`, `support_view_document`, `support_view_photo`, `support_view_evidence`, `support_view_team`, `support_view_invitations`, `support_member_access`, `support_invitation_resend`, `support_invitation_cancel`, `support_archive_job`, `support_restore_job`, `support_view_archived_jobs`), `target` (record IDs only, such as `job/<inspection id>` or `member/<uid>`) and `created_at`. Member changes also record the new role and status. Entries never contain customer names, contact details, emails or job contents. Browser database access to the log stays denied by firestore.rules; there is no in-app log viewer yet.

Every superadmin tool refuses any caller without an active `_platform_admins` record with 403, before reading the target, so a refused caller cannot probe which companies exist. That includes owners, office staff, estimators, disabled members, members of suspended companies, accounts without a membership and inactive superadmins. Refused requests return no data, make no change and write no audit entry. Firebase custom claims, extra request fields and request headers cannot grant superadmin status. `tests/test_workspaces.py` holds this refusal matrix, and its route inventory check fails when an `/api/admin` route is added without a matrix entry.

## Owner logins with a temporary password

A superadmin can create a company owner's login directly instead of sending an owner invitation. In **Companies**, **Create a company** offers **Owner sign-in: Create login with temporary password**, and **Manage company** offers **Create login with temporary password** beside **Invite / replace owner**. The superadmin enters the owner's email (the username) and a temporary password they type, of 12 to 128 characters. The field is masked; **Show** reveals it to check before creating the login, and **Hide** masks it again. After creation the page shows the email and that password once with **Copy password**, even if refreshing the company page afterwards fails, but only while the account that submitted the form is still signed in; if the browser switched accounts meanwhile (for example in another tab), it shows nothing. A sign-in change also removes a shown card and clears typed temporary passwords, and while it is still applying the form refuses to submit with "Your sign-in changed. Reload and try again." Switching **Create a company** back to **Email an invitation** clears the temporary password. The app sends no email: the superadmin sends the sign-in from their own email. Owners keep using invitations for estimators and office staff.

`POST /api/admin/companies` with `owner_password`, or `POST /api/admin/companies/{id}/owner-login` with `email`, `password` and the company's `expected_version`, does this in one guarded operation:

1. It refuses any caller who is not an active superadmin, before any lookup, so a refused request touches no Firebase account and writes no record or audit entry. The `/api/admin` refusal matrix in `tests/test_workspaces.py` covers both routes.
2. It refuses, with a message and no change, an email that already has a company membership, a Google account (with or without a password), a disabled Firebase account, a password outside 12 to 128 characters, a suspended or unknown company and a stale `expected_version`.
3. A transaction reserves the account in `_owner_login_reservations` (keyed by the email's SHA-256, holding a random claim and a 600-second expiry). It refuses an account that already has a membership (including one from an accepted invitation) or an unexpired reservation from another owner login. While the reservation stands, invitation acceptance for that email returns 409, so no competing request can take the account between the password write and the membership commit. Any failure after this step deletes the request's own reservation; an abandoned one expires. The expiry outlasts the request's Firebase writes, so a slow write cannot land after another owner login reused the account: those writes go through a separate Firebase app (`adr-owner-login`) with a 10-second HTTP timeout and SDK retries turned off (the SDK would otherwise retry 500 and 503 responses and honor Retry-After waits of up to six hours). Each call is at most three rounds of a token fetch and the request (google-auth retries a 401 twice), each a 10-second connect and read wait, so at most 120 seconds; an owner login makes at most two calls, 240 seconds, well inside 600. A transient Firebase error therefore fails the request instead of retrying it, and the superadmin tries again. An unexpected Firebase or database failure, such as the missing permission below, answers 503 "The owner login could not be created. Try again or contact support."; the temporary-password change answers 503 "Your password could not be changed. Try again." Other routes keep the generic draft-retry message.
4. It then creates the email/password account in Firebase Authentication, or sets the password on an existing account without a membership (such as one an owner invitation provisioned), marks the email verified because the superadmin vouches for it, and sets the custom claim `password_change_required`. A new account is created unverified and becomes verified in the same update that sets the claim, so it is never usable without the claim. Setting a password ends the account's other sessions.
5. One transaction then rechecks superadmin status, that the reservation is still this request's, the company's status and version and that the account still has no membership, and deletes the reservation when it commits. It makes the account the active owner with the semantics of an accepted owner invitation: the prior owner's membership is disabled, pending owner invitations and pending invitations to either owner's email are cancelled, and the company's `owner_uid` and version change. An owner invitation accepted in between makes this a 409 conflict; the Firebase account then holds the temporary password but grants nothing, and a retry with the new version reuses it.
6. The same transaction appends an `owner_login_created` entry to `_admin_events`: the superadmin's uid, the company, `target` `member/<uid>`, and `details` with the masked email (first character and domain, such as `n***@example.com`), `replacement`, `previous_owner_uid` and the number of cancelled invitations. It is the one admin entry that holds an email, and only masked.

The password goes only to Firebase Authentication. It is never logged, stored in Firestore, put in an audit entry or returned in a response; validation errors (422) carry each problem's message and location but never the submitted values, and the response carries only the company and the owner's uid and email.

**Must change password.** The new membership carries `password_change_required: true`, the server's gate. Until it clears, the membership grants nothing: the account has no role, capabilities or superadmin support access, and every session, job, workspace, signing-link and Live route answers 403 "Set your own password before using DryScope." (Live refuses before any model connection). Only `POST /api/account/password` is open to it. The `password_change_required` custom claim is a denial condition too: every route except the password change refuses a token carrying it with the same 403, before any membership lookup, so an account whose membership step failed after Firebase took the temporary password cannot accept an invitation or use any other route with it. The browser reads the custom claim: after sign-in with the claim, the app shows only **Set your own password** (new password twice, at least 12 characters) and makes no session, job or workspace calls. If the token predates the claim, the session's 403 shows the same step. On save, the backend sets the new password and removes the claim in Firebase Authentication first, then clears the membership marker and appends `temporary_password_replaced`; the app reauthenticates the same account with the new password and opens the owner's dashboard. Because the backend sets the password itself, a modified client cannot clear the gate without changing it. If Firebase fails, the gate stays and the owner retries. If the request fails after Firebase may have taken the new password (a 5xx or a lost response), the app tries reauthenticating the same account with the new password; when that works, it resumes with that sign-in, and if the server's marker is still set the owner saves once more to finish. If the browser signed in to another account while the save was pending (for example in another tab), the app opens nothing and leaves that sign-in alone. The route requires a password sign-in and either the marker or the claim, so an account whose membership step failed can still choose a password; once set, a password changes only through **Forgot password**, which keeps working for these accounts. Forgot password before the change does not lift the gate: the owner still sets a password in the app.

Release needs the runtime role to hold `firebaseauth.users.update` (see [firebase-isolation.md](firebase-isolation.md)).

## Sign-in methods

With WORKSPACES_ENABLED=true the backend accepts Firebase ID tokens whose sign-in provider is `google.com` or `password`, and only when the email is verified. Password accounts are created by invitation or, for owners, by a superadmin with a temporary password (see above). The app has no sign-up control. Before each invitation email is sent, the backend looks up the invited email in Firebase Authentication and creates an unverified account without a password if none exists. This stops anyone else from registering the invited address first. If the account does not sign in with Google, the email carries a set-password link; otherwise it tells the invitee to sign in with Google using that email. Resending an invitation reuses the account and mails a fresh link. The link is a credential, so it appears only in the outgoing email, never in Firestore or logs. If the link has expired, the invitee uses **Forgot password** on the sign-in page. An unverified password account stays on the verification step and makes no session or job calls. A verified account still has no access until it accepts a company invitation for that exact email. The legacy STAFF_EMAILS mode is unchanged.

Public sign-up must stay disabled, or the Firebase Auth API still accepts self-registration with the public web API key. It was disabled on October 10, 2026 (see `docs/review-repairs.md`) and read back as disabled on October 14, 2026. Re-applying it is a separate production change. Run it only with operator approval:

```
PATCH https://identitytoolkit.googleapis.com/admin/v2/projects/alert-disaster-field-2026/config?updateMask=client.permissions.disabledUserSignup
{"client":{"permissions":{"disabledUserSignup":true}}}
```

Verify it by reading `GET https://identitytoolkit.googleapis.com/admin/v2/projects/alert-disaster-field-2026/config` and confirming `client.permissions.disabledUserSignup` is `true`. While sign-up is disabled, a client sign-up attempt returns `auth/admin-restricted-operation`, which the app shows as "Ask your company owner for an invitation." Server-side account creation through the Admin SDK is not affected.

Identity Platform request logging is on (`monitoring.requestLogging.enabled=true`). It was enabled on October 16, 2026, with operator approval, after a password-reset email arrived that no log could attribute: until then Firebase Auth recorded no sign-in, sign-up or reset request anywhere. Each Identity Toolkit request, including `sendOobCode` password-reset requests sent from **Forgot password** or straight to the public API, is now written to Cloud Logging in `alert-disaster-field-2026`. Each entry records the request method and its outcome together with the email address involved and the caller's IP address, so these logs hold personal data; read them only to investigate sign-in, reset or account-security events. The change was applied alone with `PATCH …/config?updateMask=monitoring.requestLogging.enabled` and `{"monitoring":{"requestLogging":{"enabled":true}}}`, and a read-back confirmed every other config field was unchanged. Turning it off is a separate approved production change with the same mask and `false`.

**Forgot password** asks "Send a password reset link to name@…?" before it sends anything, because it sits just above **Sign in with Google** and a stray tap would otherwise email a reset link.

The app runs only on its canonical host. A host written with a trailing dot (`alert-disaster-field-2026.web.app.`) is a separate browser origin that Firebase Auth refuses for Google sign-in (`auth/unauthorized-domain`) and the Live relay refuses (HTTP 403), so the app and the signing page reload on the same URL without the dot before Firebase starts. A Home Screen app added from the dotted address stays on that origin, and iOS opens the canonical page in a sheet above it, so the app also tells the user to delete the icon and add it again from the correct address. Local drafts are stored per origin, so while any draft on the dotted address has not synced the app stays there, shows that drafts are still syncing and to keep the icon, and moves to the canonical host once the queue is empty.

Firebase keeps one account per email. Signing in with Google replaces an unverified email/password account with the same address, and some combinations return "This email already uses a different sign-in method." Each staff member should use one sign-in method per email. The app has no account-linking flow.

`firebase.json` enables the email/password provider. The project already has it enabled alongside Google (read back on October 14, 2026), so activation needs no auth deploy. Re-applying the `firebase.json` providers is a separate approved step: `firebase deploy --only auth --project alert-disaster-field-2026`. The Firebase CLI auth deploy only enables providers; it also re-sends the existing Google provider settings from `firebase.json`. Those settings name the Google sign-in consent screen `DryScope` (`oAuthBrandDisplayName`); the live consent screen shows that name only after this approved auth deploy runs.

## Invitations and interfaces

Superadmin creates companies or sends owner replacements. Owners invite estimator/office emails; superadmin support can resend or cancel those invitations but not create them. Invitations last seven days and activate only after explicit acceptance by a verified account with the exact invited email. For owner cleanup controls, see the in-app **Team and invitations** help. The owner-only clear endpoint requires a connection and clears up to 400 eligible invitations per request. It sets `cleared_at` and `cleared_by` in one transaction and does not delete the record. Pending unexpired invitations and accepted invitations are unchanged, so acceptance checks, the daily invitation quota and the audit history keep working as before. An invitation already cleared or accepted by the time the transaction reads it is skipped. Resending a cleared expired invitation renews its expiry and restores its list visibility in the same transaction. Acceptance checks current inviter access and company status. Owner invitations bind the owner being replaced so competing invitations cannot silently replace a newly accepted owner. Repeated acceptance is idempotent; incompatible existing membership, expiry and cancellation return conflicts. Team changes use expected versions.

Invitation email uses one of two server-only transports, chosen by `INVITATION_TRANSPORT`: exactly `resend` (the default when unset or empty) or `smtp` (Gmail with a Google app password). Both send the same subject (`Invitation to join <company name> on DryScope`), text and HTML, with up to three attempts on transient failures. The HTML is branded with the company's name and its Company settings logo (without one, the DryScope logo, bundled in `backend/brand/` because the build context is `backend/`), says what DryScope is, who sent it and that the recipient can ignore an unexpected invitation. The logo travels as an inline attachment (`content_id` for Resend, `multipart/related` for SMTP), never as a data URL or hosted image; company text is HTML-escaped. Send failures are visible; operators can resend or cancel. A send reserves its attempt for 30 seconds, and concurrent sends within that window reuse it. Attempts older than 23 hours require explicit resend, which keeps Resend inside its idempotency window. Any other transport value (including different capitalization or surrounding spaces) or a missing setting fails the delivery before any account is provisioned.

- **Resend** needs `INVITATION_FROM` (a sender on a domain verified in Resend) and `RESEND_API_KEY`. Each attempt has an eight-second transport deadline. Each attempt carries the idempotency key `<invitation id>/<send attempt>`, so Resend suppresses a duplicate when a retry repeats a request it already accepted.
- **Gmail SMTP** connects to `smtp.gmail.com` on port 587 and upgrades with STARTTLS before signing in. Each network operation has an eight-second timeout, and the whole send, from the start of account provisioning through every attempt and pause, stops after 24 seconds, inside the 30-second reservation, so a concurrent send cannot open a second Gmail session for the same attempt. If account provisioning uses up that time, the delivery fails without contacting Gmail and the operator can resend. It needs `INVITATION_FROM`, `INVITATION_SMTP_USERNAME` (the Gmail address) and `INVITATION_SMTP_PASSWORD` (the app password). Set `INVITATION_FROM` to that same Gmail address, for example `DryScope <sender@example.com>`; Gmail rewrites any other From address unless it is a verified "Send mail as" alias. At release, an operator changes the display name in the production `INVITATION_FROM` secret to DryScope; this change does not update it. SMTP has no idempotency key. Each attempt sets the deterministic Message-ID `<invitation-<invitation id>-<send attempt>@<sender domain>>`, which is the stored delivery id. Gmail does not deduplicate on it, so duplicate suppression is weaker than Resend's: if a connection fails after Gmail accepted the message, the retry can deliver a second copy. Mail clients may group the copies by their shared Message-ID. Google limits a personal Gmail account to about 500 recipients per day and may block sending for up to 24 hours after that. The app's own cap of 50 invitations per company per day sits below that limit for a single company. Every email the service sends, invitations and document deliveries alike, adds to one shared daily count (`_email_counts/daily`, a 24-hour window from its first send); a failed send is not counted. Past 400, each further send logs a warning (`emails_today`, never an address) and `/api/access` returns `email_notice: true` to every company's owner, whose dashboard then says invitations may not send until tomorrow. Nothing is refused at that point; Gmail's own limit still applies.

Before switching to Gmail, the Google account owner turns on 2-Step Verification, then creates an app password for that account at `https://myaccount.google.com/apppasswords`. The owner stores it in Secret Manager themselves as a dedicated secret (in alert-disaster-field-2026 it is named `ADRfieldappinvitations`), never in chat, source, CLI arguments or artifacts. Bind it to the service as the environment variable `INVITATION_SMTP_PASSWORD`. Changing the Google account password revokes app passwords, so a new one must replace the secret version. To switch to Resend later, verify a sending domain in Resend, store `RESEND_API_KEY` in its own secret and bind it, set `INVITATION_FROM` to a sender on that domain, and set `INVITATION_TRANSPORT=resend` (or remove it). The SMTP settings can then be removed and the app password revoked.

The invitation link is an opaque invitation ID; the ID alone grants no account access. Account provisioning needs the backend runtime identity to read and create Firebase Authentication users and generate password-reset links. The runtime account holds the custom role `adrInviteProvisioner` (`firebaseauth.users.get`, `firebaseauth.users.create`, `firebaseauth.users.sendEmail`) for this; the first real invitation on the staged revision is the live confirmation. No custom-token grant is required. A failed lookup, creation or link generation marks the delivery failed; **Resend invitation** retries it.

## Document delivery

`backend/delivery.py` holds the delivery of signed paperwork (roadmap Phase 2). For new signatures, on site (`POST /api/authorize`) or by link (`POST /api/sign`), the server creates one delivery record atomically with the signature in the same transaction; a queue failure prevents the signature from committing. Sending starts only after that durable receipt. It emails the homeowner the signed proposal and photo log when the signature recorded `email_copy=true` and the coordination record's `customer_email` is a valid address. The message is a private read-only link (`/documents#…`), with no document attached; sender identity is described under **Email** below. Adjuster email uses the signed claim snapshot and a separate private link to the itemized claim, defense letter and scope-code JSON; missing or invalid adjuster emails are skipped with a reason. Crew-lead entries follow the company settings below. A send failure leaves the signature saved and the homeowner entry failed and retryable; repeating the same signing request retries that entry and never sends a second copy after one succeeds. An uncertain send is not retried. Signatures without the email choice skip homeowner delivery. Nothing scans or backfills older signed jobs. With no customer email, or with the box unticked, the homeowner entry is skipped and the reason is stored on the entry. The send runs on the server after the receipt: the phone does not queue it, and signing with no connection is refused before any email. Owners and estimators who can sign the job trigger it by signing; office staff, superadmin support and another company's user cannot sign, so they cannot queue a delivery. Status reads and Resend use the `delivery_resend` capability described below.

- **Sender.** Delivery uses the invitation sender (`send_email` in `backend/workspaces.py`): the same `INVITATION_TRANSPORT`, `INVITATION_FROM` and secrets, the 24-second deadline and retries for definite rejections. Document deliveries stop retrying after uncertain provider acceptance; invitation retries are unchanged. Its Message-ID is `<delivery-<delivery id>-<role>-<attempt>@<sender domain>>` and its Resend idempotency key `<delivery id>/<role>/<attempt>`. Invitations are unchanged.
- **Record.** One private sidecar per signed job, `_job_deliveries/<job id>`, beside its coordination record and never inside the signed agreement. It names the job's authorization and company and holds one entry each for `homeowner`, `adjuster` and `crew_lead`: `queued`, `sending`, `sent`, `failed` or `skipped`, with the reason. Queueing again returns the first record unchanged.
- **Retry.** A send handles queued entries and retryable failures; sent and skipped entries are never sent again, so repeating a send is the retry. Each entry reserves its attempt for 30 seconds, and a concurrent send skips it. Gmail does not deduplicate, so after the 30-second reservation expires, status reads for owners, office staff and superadmin support show an attempt without a recorded outcome as `failed`, with a reason saying it may have arrived and no retry allowed. Reading status does not send mail or rewrite the sidecar; a later send persists that failure. The same non-retryable failure applies when SMTP disconnects or times out during or after sending, or Resend returns an uncertain outcome.
- **Email.** Each document-delivery message names the company with its logo, name, address and phone, sets Reply-To to the company's contact email when that is a valid address (otherwise it has none), and ends "Powered by DryScope". The Phase 2 step 1 PR applies this identity requirement only to document-delivery emails; invitation headers and templates stay unchanged, with no new Reply-To.
- **Logs.** Each entry change logs only the delivery ID, recipient role and status.
- **Status and Resend.** Owners and office staff (`delivery_resend` in `can()` and on the server) see each recipient's status on the signed job and can Resend. Resend calls `POST /api/jobs/{inspection_id}/delivery/resend` and sends only queued entries and retryable failures; sent and skipped entries are never sent again. The button needs a connection and says so when the phone is offline. Estimators, another company's user, and superadmin support are refused (403 or 404). Superadmin support sees the same status, with no addresses, on the read-only signed agreement (`delivery` on `GET /api/admin/companies/{id}/authorizations/{auth_id}`) and the server has no support Resend route. A job signed before this record exists, or a company whose delivery was off at signing, has no entries to send; nothing is backfilled. The phone does not queue mail. The changed app shell files are already in the service-worker precache; the committed worker version stays `unstamped`, and a Hosting deploy stamps the commit into that cache name.

- **Crew lead and the company switch.** Company settings (owner only; `delivery_manage` in `can()` and on the server) stores a crew-lead email and **Send document emails after signing**. Both live on the company profile and are left off signed documents, so a proposal never prints the email or the switch. Omitting them on a save keeps the stored values. A company saved before the fields existed, and a new company, has delivery off and no crew-lead email; absent reads as off and nothing is backfilled onto old records. No company is switched on in code. After a real send is tested, the ADR owner turns the switch on in Company settings. While it is off, queueing marks homeowner, adjuster and crew lead skipped with the reason that delivery is turned off, and a send of entries queued earlier skips any that are still unsent. While it is on, a missing crew-lead email skips that role; otherwise the crew-lead entry is queued for owner or office Resend (automatic crew sending is still pending); the crew-lead message is a job summary of the address, rooms, equipment and drying days and contains no prices, notes or transcripts. Homeowner sending follows the recorded checkbox; adjuster sending uses the signed claim snapshot. Saving company settings needs a connection; offline, the fields are disabled. Estimators, office staff, superadmin support and another company's user cannot change the settings.

## Company prices

`PRICE_BOOKS_ENABLED` defaults off. It gates the owner-facing editor, publication, and import/export: while it is off, quote JSON on a rate book omits the price fingerprint and the price routes answer 404 (a non-superadmin support call still answers 403). While it is off, the app also shows the v1 form, including for a pinned draft a phone already holds; that draft's stored table, pin and quantities stay untouched and the item list returns when the flag is on. While it is off, a new job is neither seeded nor pinned to a v2 table when it is created; it pins at its first audit. Pricing is the same with the flag on or off, on either calculation: a job pinned to a v2 table still prices from it. Each company sets its own prices and there is no default: a company with no published book cannot price an estimate, so its audit answers 409 with "Set your company prices before your first estimate" for the owner and "Ask your company owner" for everyone else, and its price export is a template with item names and units but blank prices. ADR alone starts from its own book, which `backend/adr_price_book.py` holds and `scripts/migrate_price_books.py` writes. A company other than ADR needs the flag on to set prices, and turning the flag on in production is a separate release with the owner's word. A v2 draft pins its table at its first server save (a role that cannot audit leaves it unpinned), a v1 draft pins its book at its first audit, and every new audit records that price basis. While it is on, an owner edits the 21 unit prices (the catalog rates plus the additional-work hourly rate) from Company prices on `adr-residential-v1`, or a full owner-defined v2 price table (`calculation_version: 'dryscope-v2'`, `schema_version: 2`) holding up to 150 `items` and a company `policy`. On `adr-residential-v1`, formulas, factors, default quantities, and mandatory wording stay fixed, there is no minimum charge, and zero is allowed only for the trip charge and the after-hours job charge, and only with an explicit acknowledgement.

In `dryscope-v2`, each item carries `item_id`, `code`, `group` (`job_charges`, `demolition`, `treatment`, `drying_equipment`, `labor`, `optional_services`, `extras`), `name`, `customer_description`, `unit` (`sf`, `lf`, `each`, `hour`, `day`, `unit_day`, `load`, `visit`), `quantity_source` (closed grammar parsed by `price_table.parse_source`: `room:demolition:unfinished`, `room:demolition:finished`, `room:floor`, `room:wet_floor`, `room:basis`; `field:` with `emergency_calls`, `monitoring_hours`, `haul_loads`, `air_scrubbers`, `hepa_filters`, `matterport_scans`, `dump_truck_loads`, `equipment_decon`, `power_distribution` or `labor_hours`; `count_days:air_movers*drying_days`, `count_days:dehumidifiers*drying_days`, `count_days:wood_floor_systems*wood_floor_days`; `after_hours`; `typed`; `typed_count_days`), `unit_price`, `allow_zero`, `default_quantity` (a whole number for count and count-days sources and for every `field:` source except `monitoring_hours` and `labor_hours`), `waste_percent`, `categories` (`1`, `2`, `3`), `taxable`, `xactimate_code`, `enabled`, and `sort_order`. The company `policy` carries `estimate_mode` (`range` or `single`), `area_basis` (`demolition` or `wet_floor`), `range` (`first_sf`, `first_percent`, `beyond_percent` with `beyond_percent <= first_percent`), `after_hours` (`multiplier` 1.000–2.000 and owner-editable plain-text `label` defaulting to `"After-hours adjustment"`), `category_multipliers` (`1`, `2`, `3`), `cabinetry` (`multiplier` defaulting to `1.000` because new companies start with no multipliers, `scope`), optional `minimum_job_charge` (`0` when off, applied before overhead-and-profit and sales tax), `overhead_profit_percent` (applied before sales tax: when overhead and profit is on, the sales-tax base for taxable items includes their proportional share of overhead and profit; when overhead and profit is off, it plays no part in tax), `sales_tax` (`enabled`, `percent`), and `show_line_breakdown`. `starter_table()` is the vendor-neutral starter catalog: 35 items across all 7 groups with blank prices and nothing from ADR's book. A company with no published book other than ADR gets that table from `GET /api/companies/{id}/prices` with `starter: true`, `calculation_version: null` and `version_id: 'unset'`, alongside blank `rates` rows that remain only for API compatibility and the v1 template (the editor reads `items`), and its export is the blank v1 template with `base_version_id` `unset`; `version_id=starter` on the export route gives the v2 template with `base_version_id` `starter` and no rates, and only that v2 file can be uploaded: a v1 rates preview or v1 upload from a company with no published book answers 409, because a new company has no rate-book path. Nothing is written until the owner saves a working copy or publishes. ADR keeps the v1 view and template. A company with no published book has no prices, so its audit answers 409 until it publishes. A company whose current published book is `adr-residential-v1` previews or publishes a v2 table only through its owner's switch (`POST .../prices/start-v2`, below), and cannot upload a v2 template; a company with no published book may publish v2. Audits, additional-work quotes and approvals, and reprice price a job from the v2 table pinned to it; a v1 rates preview on a v2 company answers 409. A new job takes the table's default quantities once and never again when it is opened: on the phone at New job, or on the server when office staff create it. `seeded_from_version` records the table. A job begun before the company had a table keeps the quantities it has. Jobs store custom item quantities in `scope_items` and estimator overrides of walkthrough-measured quantities in `quantity_overrides` (recording `measured` and `entered` from the walkthrough fill onward, and a short `reason` that saves and audits require while the two differ; `entered` must match the job's current value). Item names, customer descriptions, and the after-hours label cannot start with `=`, `+`, `-` or `@`, so exports stay spreadsheet-safe and round-trip unchanged.

The policy may also hold an optional `assist` map: `drying_days` (whole number, 1 to 60), `range_method` (`cushion` or `uncertainty`), `extra_drying_days` (whole number, 0 to 30) and `hidden_damage_percent` (`1`, `2`, `3`, each 0 to 100, at most two decimals). A table stored without it keeps its hash: `normalize_policy` never adds the map, and readers go through `price_table.assist_of`, where absence reads as 3 days, the cushion, 0 extra days and 0 percent. The starter table carries the uncertainty method with 1 extra day and 10 / 15 / 25 percent, and a table mapped from a rate book keeps the cushion so its range matches the rate book. `drying_days` is what a new job starts at. The range fields are stored only, and do not change a price, until the uncertainty range ships. See [Estimate assist](estimate-assist.md).

Publishing takes effect immediately at a server timestamp. It does not change a saved draft, a signed agreement, or the company identity version. While editing, the owner's unpublished working copy lives in `_price_book_drafts/{company_id}` (`PUT /api/companies/{id}/prices/working-copy`, `DELETE /api/companies/{id}/prices/working-copy` or `POST /api/companies/{id}/prices/working-copy/discard`), is never used to price a job, and is cleared on publish. A working copy may leave enabled items unpriced. Publishing needs a price on every enabled item and at least one such item, and a zero price needs `allow_zero` and an acknowledgement. `POST /api/companies/{id}/prices/calculate` is owner-only and returns an unsaved table's publish problems and what the sample jobs cost on it (`samples`). The owner reviews one draft and applies the new prices to that draft; estimators cannot reprice, and there is no bulk reprice. Restoring a version across calculation versions (`adr-residential-v1` vs `dryscope-v2`) is refused with HTTP 409. A v2 draft's first server save and a v1 draft's first audit write the pin. A draft created before the company's price books started (its first publication, or the price-book migration), or one that already has an audit, keeps ADR's starting book (including a customized additional-work rate) instead of jumping to a later book when the company is ADR. Any other company's draft pins its current book, unless it was created before that company's switch to v2, when it pins the company's last rate book. Copies do not copy the pin.

**Price editor (`public/prices.js`, owner, online only).** For a company on `dryscope-v2` (or one with nothing published, which reads as the starter list) the Prices page is an editor. The page loads `GET /api/companies/{id}/prices`: the published `items` and `policy`, plus the owner's `working_copy` when one is saved. A company with nothing published gets `starter: true` (the vendor-neutral starter items, blank prices, default settings) built in `PriceBookStore._view` in `backend/price_book_store.py`; nothing is written until the owner edits. Every edit autosaves the whole table to the working copy about 0.7 s after the last keystroke (`PUT .../working-copy` with `expected_working_version` and `expected_generation`); a stale version answers 409 "Prices changed on another device. Reload." and the item and settings controls and the bar's buttons lock until the owner reloads, so nothing is typed that cannot be saved and another device's saved copy cannot be discarded unseen. A saved copy is never used for pricing: jobs price only from a published version. The **Unpublished changes** bar shows whenever the saved copy differs from the published table (or edits are still waiting to save) and offers **Publish** (reason, review, publish, as for any price change) and **Discard changes** (`POST .../working-copy/discard`, which first stops any save still waiting). Settings the owner sets here: estimate style (range or one price), area basis, the range cushion, the after-hours multiplier and its document line label, category and cabinetry multipliers, a minimum job charge (0 is off), overhead and profit, sales tax and printing the itemized lines. Overhead and profit is added before sales tax. `POST .../prices/calculate` (owner only, stateless, its own `price_calculate` allowance of 300 per user per day so sample editing cannot use up the `price_preview` allowance that review, import and restore need) returns `samples`, `SAMPLE_JOBS` priced by `pricing_v2` on the unsaved table for the sentence under the settings, and `problems`, what publishing would reject. Items with no price are left out and the sample says so (`incomplete`); a sample none of the priced items touches is omitted. Every preview stores `sample_before` (the published table) and `sample_after`, which the review shows. Samples never price a saved job, and each carries that notice. Each changed item in a v2 diff lists its changed `fields` with before and after values (`diff_tables` in `backend/price_table.py`), and the review shows them, for example "waste 0% -> 50%"; a reader's diff leaves out the fields a reader cannot see. Any edit closes a review on screen, so Publish always publishes what the owner last reviewed; publishing an import or restore replaces the working copy, and its review says so. An import with a zero price needs the form's confirmation (`confirm_zero_prices`), which acknowledges the file's zero rows. Reopening the page saves any edit still waiting first. Nothing in the editor enters the offline draft queue; offline the inputs are disabled. An edit caught by a dropped connection stays in memory, shows "Not saved", and saves when the connection returns if the editor is still on screen or the owner reopens Prices; a reload loses it. Estimators and office staff open the same page read-only; the superadmin support view is read-only and audited.

**Switch to v2 (owner, online only).** A company on `adr-residential-v1` keeps its rate page, and its owner also sees a **Switch to v2** card that says what will change. `POST .../prices/start-v2` (`prices_manage`, audited as `price_switch_started`) saves a working copy mapped from the company's own current rates, or the blank starter list if it has none of its own, and answers 409 if a working copy exists or the company is already on a table. A company's rates never reach another company. While priced extras are off the additional-work item starts switched off. The page then opens the editor with the rate page's import and restore put away; jobs still price from the rate book. The review (`switch` on the preview) lists the differences in plain words, what the owner changed from the mapped table, and sample jobs priced on both calculations. Publishing moves new jobs to the table; discarding returns to the rate page. A draft begun earlier keeps its rate book, pinned or not, and shows the v1 form, online or offline, until the owner chooses **Review new prices for this job**, whose review lists the same differences and the draft's before and after totals. The session carries `switched_at` so a phone can tell offline. `created_at` is the server's time of a job's first save. For a job the server has not seen, the phone keeps `started_at` on its own draft row, never sent to the server, so a job begun after the switch shows the item list before its first save, with no default quantities if the table could not load or the phone's copy is out of date. One limit, accepted: a draft begun before the publish and still only on a phone, or a row from before `started_at` existed, shows the v1 form offline, then at its first save becomes a new job on the published table, with the item list and no reprice review. Nothing is signed by then and no quantity is lost. The card and the review therefore tell the owner to have phones sync open drafts before publishing. A signed job with no pin also stays on the rate-book view, with nothing newer or pending to report. See [Switching from a rate book to a table](pricing-source.md#switching-from-a-rate-book-to-a-table).

Import and export use a fixed CSV/XLSX template (`dryscope-prices-v1` for v1 rate books; `dryscope-prices-v2` with 20 item columns for v2 tables). The template carries no policy, so a v2 upload keeps the published policy, or the starter default when none is published, and never takes it from the working copy. The preview the owner confirms is the snapshot that publish stores. Failed imports change nothing. Additional work stays locked when priced extras are off, so a spreadsheet cannot turn that feature on: a v2 preview or publish with an enabled `extras` item is refused until the owner turns that item off, and a working copy may keep the item and reports it as a publish problem. Price-only changes are audited in `_admin_events` with `domain` `pricing`. The private collections `_company_price_books`, `_price_book_versions`, `_job_pricing`, `_price_book_previews`, and `_price_book_drafts` are server-only, like the other company collections. `scripts/migrate_price_books.py` accepts only `--company adr`, is a dry run until `--apply`, and requires the dedicated project and a manifest; see [ADR price book](../RELEASES.md#adr-price-book). It does not otherwise change inspections, and it does not write authorizations, evidence, or identity versions.
