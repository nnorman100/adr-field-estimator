# Access matrix: Estimate and review

Part of [Access matrix](../ACCESS.md).

## Estimate and review (rebuilt app, S5a-9)

`web/src/lib/estimates/`, two handlers on one path, `/api/jobs/{id}/estimate`, both `requireFlag('price_books', ...)` (a company without price books gets 404) and both through the job-access function above. No new capability key. Reprice (S5b-6) is a third handler, `/api/jobs/{id}/reprice`, also `requireFlag('price_books', ...)`, with the owner's key `job_reprice`. Only Reprice takes a body: the company is the caller's own from the sign-in, the record is the job's own.

| Route | Who | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| `POST /api/jobs/{id}/estimate` | `audit` (owner, estimator) | Prices the job's submitted record (the committed submission of its current draft) and builds the four documents, all in one transaction under the job's row lock. With no pin yet, the job is pinned to the company's current published version ("pin at first price"); only the owner's Reprice (S5b-6) ever moves a pin. The record is priced from the pinned version; the documents are built from the record (its address, rooms, photos, customer, claim and written summary) and the company's profile, and the proposal is stored as bytes with its SHA-256 for signing to bind. 201 the first time, 200 and the same estimate for the same record and pin again (a retry, or a second phone); a later publish never changes it. 409 `prices_not_set` with no published version, `not_submitted` for a job being edited, `job_signed`, `scope_not_confirmed` and `summary_missing` for a record without them, `estimate_refused` with the engine's stage and sentence, `record_unreadable`; 503 `prices_unreadable` for a pinned version this server cannot price, `settings_unreadable` for stored company document settings that do not read through their schema. The photo audit log lists the photos in the company's own `photo_log_grouping` (`room`, the default, or `shot_tag`), read from its own row only. A refusal writes nothing, the pin included. |
| `POST /api/jobs/{id}/reprice` | `job_reprice` (owner only; S5b-6) | Reprice this draft. Body `{ revision, changeId, versionId }` (Zod, strict): the job revision the owner saw, this change's own id, and the price version the owner reviewed. Under the company row lock and the caller's active owner membership lock (taken in that order, as the operator's owner replacement takes them, so an owner replacement cannot commit between the check and the write, and the two cannot deadlock), the job's row lock and the company's working copy lock (the lock Publish takes, so a publish cannot land between the check and the write), it applies only that version: it must still be the company's newest, and the job's revision the one the owner saw. It moves the job's price pin with one new pin row (reason `owner_reprice`), advances the job's revision (so a confirmation made before the move is stale), and writes one audit row `job_repriced` (new pin sequence, previous and new version sequence, the change id, the request's revision and version id, and the job state answered; never a price). The same `changeId` with the same body again is answered exactly as the first time, whatever has happened to the job since, with nothing written; the same `changeId` with another revision or version is 409 `conflict`. The estimate of the old pin is then no longer the job's current one, so `GET` answers `estimate: null` and it cannot be signed. A reprice clears the scope confirmation (pricing plan 3): the next `POST /estimate` is refused `scope_not_confirmed` for a record of the draft that was the job's current one when it ran (submitted, or still open: a job back in editing may be repriced too, and Finish on that open draft is not priced), and Back to editing from such a record starts the new draft with scope unconfirmed (the old confirmation is not copied), so scope is confirmed again on a draft made after the reprice and Finish; and the revised estimate needs a new signature, since a signature is bound to the proposal it was captured for. Answer: `{ job, pin: { sequence, version } }`. 400 `invalid_request`; 409 `stale_revision` (the job moved since the owner's copy), `prices_superseded` (the version named is not the company's newest), `prices_unchanged` (the job is already priced from it), `job_signed` on a signed job (a change after signing goes through S5b-9), `not_priced` for a job with no pin yet (its first estimate pins it), `conflict` (the change id was used with another body); 503 `prices_unreadable` for a version this server cannot price. Estimator and office are 403, another company's job 404, an archived job 410. A refusal writes nothing. |
| `GET /api/jobs/{id}/reprice` | `job_reprice` (owner only; S5b-6) | The before/after preview for the owner's Reprice banner. Writes nothing. Answers `{ job: { revision }, reprice }`: `reprice` is null for a signed job, a job never priced, or one already on the company's newest published version; otherwise `{ pinned: { version, price, refused }, newest: { versionId, version, price, refused } }`, the job's most recent committed record (the scope last submitted, not a draft being edited) priced from the pinned version and from the newest (a price is the proposal's wording, one amount or "low to high"). Only the two totals are priced: no documents are built and no company profile is read, so a missing property address or unreadable document settings never stop it. Each side has a `price` or, when the engine cannot price the record from that version, `price` null and `refused` the engine's stage; it informs the owner and adds no rule: the reprice is still the owner's to send and the server decides it. The job's row is locked, so `job.revision` and the record priced are one consistent view (Finish commits a record under the same lock) and the revision is the one the reprice must name. Under the company row lock and the caller's active owner membership lock, as the reprice takes them, so a caller disabled or replaced after the sign-in was verified reads nothing (403). Estimator and office are 403, another company's job 404, an archived job 410. |
| `GET /api/jobs/{id}/estimate` | `jobs_read` (everyone with the job, office included) | The current estimate (for the job's current record and pin) or `estimate: null`: a job being edited has none (the review of its last record is void, J4). |

A customer, claim or property address save after the estimate (the job's revision is newer than the one the stored result states, or the details had already changed between Finish and the first price) makes it need review: both routes still answer it, `POST` with 200 and nothing written, with the stored prices, the documents rebuilt from the record with the current details, `needsReview: true` and `signable` false. When a detail the documents need is now missing (the property address cleared), the answer is the stored prices with `missingDetails` ("Property address is missing") and no documents or proposal (`null`), never a refusal. Only Back to editing and Finish make a record, and an estimate, that can be signed at the new details. Otherwise both answer the estimate as stored, never recomputed: the lines (each naming its room and the price row used), totals, the proposal from its stored bytes, the itemized claim, scope defense letter, photo audit log, and the staff-only notes (the written summary, suggestions, company fields still missing, the engine's checks). The quantity-override note is in the answer only for `quantity_notes_read` (the owner); for everyone else the key is absent. A job another estimator owns, or another company's, is 404 on both, an archived job is 410, and office making an estimate is 403. A new estimate adds one audit row `estimate_created` (pin and version sequence, line count) and a first pin one row `job_price_pinned` (sequence, version sequence): ids, sequence numbers and counts only, never a price.

**Revised estimate heading (S5b-6, D6).** An estimate made for a job that already had one (after a reprice or after Back to editing) carries a `revised` line under the heading: "Revised {date}" (the day it is made, UTC, written out), or "Revised {date}: was {previous price}" when the company's `show_previous_price` document setting is on, read at the moment the estimate is made, the previous price being the job's last estimate's price as the proposal states it. A job with a signed agreement is always headed "Revised {date}: was {signed price}" from the agreement's stored price, whatever the setting (`revisedFor` takes the signed parent's price from its caller and `revisedLine` names it ahead of the setting; `make` refuses a signed job and never has one, so the signed-job workflow that supplies it is S5b-9's). The line is made once and kept only in the proposal bytes the signature binds, so a later change of the setting changes no estimate already made, and the rebuilt Review reads it back from those bytes. A first estimate has no `revised` key, so every proposal made before, and every signed one, keeps its bytes.
