# DryScope

A mobile inspection app serving Alert Disaster Restoration first, and other restoration companies as isolated company workspaces. The site's front page is the public DryScope landing page; open [the dedicated app](https://alert-disaster-field-2026.web.app/app) at `/app` and sign in with Google or a verified, invited email/password account.

Hosting, Firebase Authentication, Firestore, Cloud Run, private evidence storage and Vertex AI belong to `alert-disaster-field-2026`. The historical `alert-disaster-live` site was retired as of 2026-10-12; use this app's address. Peer sites and shared databases remain untouched. See [infrastructure and deployment](docs/firebase-isolation.md) and [repair acceptance evidence](docs/review-repairs.md).

## Company dashboards

The new build adds isolated company workspaces with superadmin, owner, estimator and office roles. Owners manage their company and team; estimators see jobs they create or are assigned; office staff coordinate jobs and view documents. Google or verified email/password sign-in uses private server-checked memberships. Password accounts are created by invitation, whose email carries a link to set the password, or for a company owner by a superadmin with a temporary password that the owner must replace at first sign-in. Public sign-up is disabled in the Firebase project and must stay disabled. Invitations require explicit acceptance by a verified account with the matching email. The initial ADR owner also has a separate superadmin role.

Company workspaces are enabled in production since October 15, 2026; the serving backend and Hosting release are recorded in [RELEASES.md](RELEASES.md). Invitations are sent by Gmail SMTP. See [workspace permissions, migration and release checks](docs/company-workspaces.md). The new dashboard does not create a blank inspection at sign-in. Company identity is saved with documents; workbook pricing remains shared and unchanged.

## Inspection workflow

Use the persistent **1. Walk & Photo / 2. Check Scope / 3. Review & Sign** step bar to move between tasks. A new job opens on Walk & Photo; Check Scope is one tap away for desk estimates. **Jobs** opens drafts and signed agreements; **Account** contains sign-out and company contact details. On a phone with a job open, one navy header row shows **← Jobs**, the job title with a short save status (such as **Saved**, **Saving…**, **Saved on phone · sync pending**, **Offline · saved on phone** or **Signed**) and **More**, which holds the job actions, Job coordination, Help and Account. Owners, estimators and office staff start jobs from **+ New job** at the top of the dashboard. The **?** (Help) button beside **Account** opens short how-tos that work offline: topics for the signed-in role come first, followed by getting started, other roles' topics and the company contact. Role only orders the text; it shows no job data or controls. Drafts save automatically (status under the job title; hover it for the revision or authorization ID); **Jobs → Save draft** (**More → Save draft** on a phone) saves on demand. On Walk & Photo and Check Scope the bottom bar offers **Finish & review**, or **Fill scope from walkthrough** on Walk & Photo while unconfirmed walkthrough notes, transcript or a running Live session exist. The bar is hidden on Review & Sign, where the signing area stays below whichever document is open, and on signed jobs. Scope confirmation is required before document generation.

1. Enter the address, confirmed year built, rooms, measured demolition areas, water categories, waste, cabinetry, equipment, drying days and additional labor. Add applicable Matterport, dump-truck, decontamination, power-distribution and wood-floor drying charges from the sheet. Describe crawl spaces, countertops, water heaters and similar job conditions in the notes or walkthrough. Documents describe the work as proposed. Insurance claims add optional **Claim & loss details** under the scope: carrier, claim and policy numbers, date of loss, cause and source of loss, adjuster name/email/phone and deductible. They are saved on the job's private coordination record, not in the signed inspection; the documents print the values saved when they were generated, and the defense letter is addressed to the recorded adjuster with the claim reference.
2. On **Walk & Photo**, tap a room chip to choose the current room (tap the selected chip to rename it, or **+ Room** to add one) and start the camera/microphone for real Gemini Live coaching. The Cloud Run relay authenticates the Firebase user and checks inspection ownership before connecting with server ADC. It sends 16 kHz mono PCM and an aspect-preserving 640 px JPEG every 2 s; responses play at 24 kHz after a short cushion. On a weak connection the camera feed pauses automatically so voice keeps flowing, and the Live status says so. Technician and assistant transcripts remain separate. Active sessions reveal Stop/Mute controls; optional color emulation and the transcript are under **More walkthrough options**; the emulation is explicitly labeled and does not produce thermal evidence. When reception is too poor for Live, use **Offline notes** (see [Offline notes for bad reception](#offline-notes-for-bad-reception--deployed-acceptance-pending)).
3. **Capture photo** takes a still while Live runs and opens the camera or file chooser otherwise; **Upload file** adds an existing photo. The photo strip under the button, and each room card on Check Scope, show every photo with its room, capture time, dimensions and recognition badge; a room menu on the card moves a photo to another room of the unsigned draft. Originals are retained privately. Pending, failed and non-meter OCR remain unverified. Recognition can be retried.
   The **photo shot list** under the room chips (and on each room card) shows the photos an adjuster expects for the current room: a room overview, each affected wall or floor and a meter reading on each wet material, plus the source of loss, contents before they are moved and equipment as placed when the technician marks the room **Source of loss is here**, **Contents in room** or **Equipment placed here**. The technician enters how many **Affected walls or floors** and **Wet materials** the room has; each needs its own tagged photo ("1 of 2"), zero asks for none, and until a count is set the list asks for one photo and names the count as not set. Right after a capture a one-tap picker tags the new photo with its shot type (the room's missing shots first; **Not now** leaves it untagged), and a shot-type menu on each photo card changes or clears the tag. Only tagged photos count; photos saved before the list existed count as untagged. Live coaching asks for the room's missing shots before moving on, may suggest a tag that the technician taps, and hears each saved tag between its replies; it never treats a shot as taken without a saved tag and asks for any count that is not set. The still-missing list counts missing shots per room. Missing shots never block signing; the photo moisture log is unchanged. The list is data in `backend/shot_list.py`, mirrored in `public/core.js`.
4. The **Drying log** card below the scope form on **Check Scope** keeps each room's readings by day (**Add reading** on a room card, or **Log reading** on **Walk & Photo** for the current room). An air reading takes temperature and relative humidity and shows the grains per pound (GPP), dew point and vapor pressure as you type, computed again and stored by the server with the standard psychrometric equations (`backend/psychrometrics.py`, pinned in `tests/test_psychrometrics.py`). Record the affected room, a dry reference room, outside air and the dehumidifier outlet on setup day and at each monitoring visit; each day then shows how far the room sits above the dry standard, the dehumidifier's grain depression against the IICRC 12 / 20 GPP marks, the vapor-pressure differential and whether outside air is dry enough for an open system, and the room shows its GPP progress from the first day. Material readings keep the meter value, unit and dry standard; thermal readings keep the ΔT, pattern and extent. Every reading records who saved it and when it was taken. Readings are a sidecar of the job: they never enter the priced revision, the proposal hash, the documents or the signature, and they stay open for monitoring visits after signing. Recording needs a connection; the log shows an explicit empty or offline state otherwise.
5. **Scan tag** beside the room chips reads a printed QR tag with the phone camera (decoded by the vendored jsQR reader); when the camera is denied or unavailable the dialog takes the code typed from the tag. A room tag printed for this job selects that room; an equipment tag records the air mover, LGR dehumidifier or air scrubber in the current room with the scan time, and a later scan in another room moves it. Scans are listed by room under **Equipment tags** on Walk & Photo and named on each room card on Check Scope. They are saved with the draft as placement records only: the priced equipment counts, the documents and the model prompts do not read them. Codes that are not DryScope tags, or room tags from another job, are refused with a message. **Make & print QR tags** prints room tags for the job and equipment tags by asset number; the QR images come from the server.
6. Confirm every pricing input and select **Finish & review**. Capture stops. The server reads the saved revision and evidence, validates AI output, applies deterministic pricing and mandatory ADR wording, and produces four documents. Provider failures show a retry error and cannot create a signable fallback.
7. Review the proposal's low–high estimate range and itemized scope total; in the app only the proposal must be opened before the technician signs, while the itemized claim, scope defense letter and photo log remain available as supporting documents. AI summary and suggestion notes appear in an estimator-only **AI review notes** callout above the document tabs, never in the customer proposal. Unpriced work, pending recognition or missing company settings block signing; the message names each item and where to fix it. Edits invalidate the audit and signature; selecting **Finish & review** again without changes returns the saved audit. Draw a signature and confirm document review; success is shown only after a durable server receipt. To let the homeowner sign on their own phone instead, choose **Send to homeowner**: the app shows a QR code, a copyable link and (where the phone supports it) the share sheet. The homeowner opens the link with no account, opens all four documents (signing unlocks only after each is opened, matching the consent statement), types their name, chooses owner or authorized representative, checks two confirmations and draws a signature. The link works once, expires after 7 days, can be revoked, and stops working as soon as anything in the inspection changes. While it is outstanding, the **Review & Sign** step checks every 10 seconds and switches to the signed agreement when the homeowner signs. After signing, the homeowner can reopen the link for 30 days to view or print the signed copy. The server sends no email or text.
8. Open **Jobs → Drafts & signed jobs** to recover a draft or restore the immutable signed agreement, all four documents, signature and evidence. Recent records appear first; use **Load older drafts** or **Load older signed jobs** for additional pages. A confirmed agreement stays saved if evidence display fails; **Retry loading saved evidence** reloads its display without signing again. **Export** contains JSON copy/download and printing. Mobile claims use readable line-item cards; desktop and printed claims retain the table. Export uses ADR internal codes without ESX compatibility claims. Saving does not dispatch technicians or send email.

## Pricing and evidence

`backend/claims.py` calculates money with Decimal and cent rounding. The populated default job charges total $1,180.20 and appear exactly once. Rates and the estimate-range formulas are traced to the user-supplied workbook in [pricing source notes](docs/pricing-source.md). Three fans and one dehumidifier for three days cost $567.00. Waste increases applicable demolition item totals. Added labor uses the workbook's $47.46/hr formula even for an after-hours loss, as confirmed by the user; after-hours estimates add $200 in job charges and apply the sheet's labor/material factor.

The customer proposal shows the sheet-derived low–high estimate, with itemized base-rate charges shown separately as its calculation detail. ADR package tiers are not in the supplied sheet and are no longer accepted for new audits. Models cannot supply prices, override totals or remove conditional clauses. AI summaries and suggestions are labeled for technician review; suggestions do not become observed evidence or authorized work.

## Equipment sizing check

Each water room's **Drying check** fields, under **More room options** on Check Scope, take three technician-confirmed inputs: the room's floor area (not its demolition area), ceiling height and IICRC water class 1-4, plus an optional **Wet floor area** that defaults to the floor area when left blank. They stay blank until stated; Fill scope from walkthrough sets them only from quoted technician statements, and Live asks for them without estimating. The **IICRC equipment sizing check** leads the **More equipment & labor** drawer, whose closed summary line names each flag; opened, it shows the server's recommended air movers and LGR dehumidification beside the entered counts, with an **Above**, **Below** or **Within range** flag and a short reason. `backend/sizing.py` computes it, separately from pricing: air movers are one per room plus one per 70 to 50 SF of wet floor (IICRC S500 initial placement, rounded up per room), and dehumidification is the whole room's cubic feet divided by the IICRC LGR chart factor (100, 50, 40, 40 for classes 1-4) in AHAM pints per day, summed across rooms and rounded up once. Dehumidifier counts are compared once the optional **Dehumidifier rating** (AHAM pints/day per unit) is entered. Wet walls above 2 ft, ceilings and wall offsets are not collected, so the air-mover range is floor-based and an **Above** flag asks the technician to note them. Mold-only rooms are outside the check; a room missing an input, or with a wet floor area larger than its floor, shows "check not available"; a room with no wet floor area (wet walls only) still counts toward dehumidification, but the air movers cannot be sized and get no flag or defense-letter placement line. The job comparison and defense-letter paragraph appear only when every water room has its inputs. The check never changes counts or prices.

## Private drafts and updates

Changes autosave to a user-specific IndexedDB queue and synchronize to Firestore when online. Photos upload individually. Reopening a draft merges evidence added on other devices and stores downloaded originals privately on this device for offline recovery and conflict copies. If an original cannot be fetched, retry online before copying that draft. Offline drafts can be edited; AI and signing require connectivity. Conflicts return 409 and preserve local work until the user restores the saved revision or creates a separate inspection. Logout stops resources and clears local drafts once they have synced. If any draft has not synced, or a job still has an offline voice note waiting to reach its transcript, sign-out stops and offers **Discard unsynced work and sign out**. Voice notes the server could not process are kept only on the phone; sign-out says so before removing them. Retained cloud drafts and agreements are not automatically deleted.

The worker caches versioned app assets and public Firebase initialization configuration. Authenticated APIs and evidence are excluded. Navigation is network-first, and the app shell is served with `Cache-Control: no-store` so a browser refresh can pick up a release. The app checks for updates when brought to the foreground and prompts before activation, preserving drafts. The **Account** menu shows the installed app version, the start of the source commit stamped at release. If an older screen persists, save the draft, open [the current field app with a fresh URL](https://alert-disaster-field-2026.web.app/app?v=ui-20261006-6), and use **Save & update** if prompted. `alertdisaster.com` is the separate public company website and is not this field app. Physical iPhone offline launch, Bluetooth routing, interruption and conversational latency remain acceptance checks until the guided device test passes.

## Landing page

`/` serves `public/index.html`, a static DryScope landing page: hero, how a job runs, a short FAQ, the four documents and the field features, written for water mitigation and disaster restoration companies and describing only what the app does today, with every word in the HTML source. **Log in** in the header, in the hero note and in the closing line is an ordinary link to `/app`, the app's sign-in screen; it says who logs in (superadmins, owners, estimators and office staff) and changes nothing about who can sign in. The small `landing.js` sends a device that already holds a Firebase session, or an old app deep link (`?invite=`, `?v=`, Firebase email actions), on to `/app`; the page renders without it. The **Request access** links and the request form appear only when `REQUEST_ACCESS_URL` at the top of `public/landing.js` holds a `mailto:` address or an `https://` page; the form is sent by the person and creates no account.

Search: the landing carries a unique title, description, canonical link, Open Graph tags and JSON-LD (Organization, SoftwareApplication and FAQPage, restating only facts visible on the page). `robots.txt` allows crawling and names `sitemap.xml`, which lists the landing page alone. `/app`, `/app/**`, `/app.html`, `/sign` and `/sign.html` are sent with `X-Robots-Tag: noindex, nofollow` (the app shell also carries a robots meta tag) and are not in the sitemap. There is no `llms.txt`: Google Search does not use one.

WebMCP (a Chrome origin trial; the API may change): where `document.modelContext` (or `navigator.modelContext`) exists, `landing.js` registers three read-only tools built from the page's own text (`dryscope_overview`, `dryscope_faq`, `dryscope_access`), and the request-access form is annotated declaratively (`toolname`, `tooldescription`, no `toolautosubmit`), so an agent can pre-fill it and the person sends it. The sign-in form is never a tool. `/app/**` and `/sign` are served with `Permissions-Policy: tools=()`, so the signed-in app and the homeowner page expose nothing; the landing keeps the default `self`. `tests/test_landing.js` checks the registrations against a stand-in model context; real-Chrome checks need `chrome://flags/#enable-webmcp-testing` or an origin-trial token, and no token is registered or committed.

Routing: `firebase.json` rewrites `/app` and `/app/**` to `public/app.html`, redirects `/app.html` to `/app` (301) and keeps `/sign` and `/api/**`; there is no catch-all rewrite, so unknown paths get `public/404.html`. The web app manifest keeps `id` `/` (the identity installed home-screen apps already have), starts at `/app` and scopes `/`, so an installed icon that opens `/` reaches the app through the landing's hand-off. The service worker precaches the landing and the app shell and falls back to the matching one offline.

### Release-helper environment

The scripts under `scripts/` run on Linux, macOS and Windows and resolve tools from the environment instead of fixed paths:

| Variable | Used by | Resolution when unset |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `ADR_GCLOUD` | `verify_live_release.py`, `record_live_release.py` | `gcloud` on `PATH` (including `gcloud.cmd` on Windows) |
| `ADR_TEST_PYTHON` | `verify_live_browser.js` | `.venv` in the repository, then `python3` (`python` on Windows) |
| `ADR_CHROME_PATH` | `manual_chrome.js` (used by `verify_live_browser.js`) | Platform install locations, then `google-chrome`/`chromium` on `PATH` |

From WSL2, `PATH` may resolve `gcloud` to the Windows SDK's shell wrapper, which takes roughly 30 seconds per call through Windows interop; point `ADR_GCLOUD` at a native Linux gcloud for a fast check. `verify_live_release.py` caches the access token until two minutes before gcloud says it expires (at most 45 minutes) and reads the Live checkpoint off the socket's event loop, so a slow lookup can no longer miss the relay's keepalive pings (uvicorn closes with 1011 "keepalive ping timeout" after 20 s + 20 s).

When gcloud or Chrome cannot be found, the helper stops with a message naming its variable. The release helper bridges the app's Live socket with Node's built-in WebSocket client, so run it with Node 22 or newer. ## Offline notes for bad reception — deployed, acceptance pending

Live stays the default walkthrough. **Offline notes** is a record-now, process-later option for basements, crawlspaces and large buildings where Live cannot hold a connection:

- When Live has shown the weak-signal status, or has been disconnected, for 25 seconds, the Walkthrough panel offers **Switch to offline notes**. It never switches by itself; **Not now** keeps Live and the offer returns only after the signal has recovered and weakened again. The app also offers the switch when the phone goes offline or Live cannot reconnect, and **Offline notes** starts the mode manually at any time. Switching stops Live with its transcript kept, and **Back to live assistant** resumes it once online.
- **Record voice note** saves audio on the phone (MP4/AAC in iPhone Safari and the Home Screen app, WebM or Ogg Opus elsewhere) every few seconds while recording, so a reload, crash or lost signal keeps what was captured. A note is limited to 10 minutes. Each note is tagged with the **Current room** chip. Photos use **Upload file** with the same room and the existing photo upload queue. **Add typed note** adds a room-tagged line to the technician notes.
- Once online, each voice note uploads, including notes for jobs that are not open; the background queue retries every minute on a weak connection. The server checks the same company and job permissions as other scope changes (owners and the job's estimators; office staff and superadmin support access cannot), sends the audio to the configured audit model for a verbatim transcript and appends it to the technician transcript as `[Offline voice note … · room · recorded … · transcribed later]`. The server does not store the audio. Retrying a note never adds it twice.
- After notes are added to the open job, **Fill scope from walkthrough** runs as before: the filled values still need review and scope confirmation. When it could not run (Live was on, or the notes were transcribed while the job was closed), Walk & Photo and Check Scope show a one-tap **N voice notes transcribed - Fill scope and check for missing items** prompt. Its panel now also lists what is still missing (no address, year built or measured area for a room, each room's missing photo shots, or drying equipment) beside the model's clarification questions, so the technician can fill gaps before leaving the site.
- The Walkthrough panel shows each note's status (saved on this phone, waiting to upload, uploading and transcribing, added to the transcript, or why the upload failed; failed uploads retry every minute) and how many voice notes and photos are still waiting to upload.
